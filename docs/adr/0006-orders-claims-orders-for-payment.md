# Orders claims an order for a payment before payments charges the card

Payments used to check its Copy of an order, charge Stripe, and then publish `payment:created`. Orders expires an unpaid order on its own clock, so a charge could succeed just as the expiry job cancelled the order: `OrderCompletion` refused the cancelled order, the ticket went back on sale, and the buyer had paid for nothing. The same happened whenever `order:cancelled` had not yet reached payments' Copy. Money cannot be taken back by an Event, and each service decided from its own state.

Orders now decides, before any money moves. `POST /api/v1/payments` saves a pending payment (with the Stripe token) and publishes `payment:requested`, answering 202. Orders moves the order from awaiting payment to **payment processing** (`OrderPaymentClaim`) and publishes `order:payment_processing`. Only then does payments charge the card for the payment that event names, and publish `payment:paid`, or `payment:failed` with the reason when the card is declined or Stripe refuses the token. `payment:paid` completes the order; `payment:failed` puts it back to awaiting payment with its original deadline, by re-publishing `order:awaiting_payment`, and schedules expiry again, because the expiry job skips an order in payment processing. The order's `lock_version` decides a claim against an expiry or the buyer's cancel that run at once: the loser retries and finds the order settled. `OrderCompletion` completes only an order claimed for that payment. The buyer follows the payment at `GET /api/v1/payments/:id`; after a decline they can pay again, since the unique index on a payment's order covers only payments that did not fail.

Events about a payment name it (`payment_id`), and the order records the payment it was last claimed for. A replayed or late `payment:requested`, `payment:failed` or `payment:paid` for an earlier attempt then changes nothing, so replaying events (see the README) cannot release an order mid-charge or re-claim a released one. Payments events are keyed by the order, so the events of one order stay in order. `payment:created` became `payment:paid`, as a payment now exists before it is paid.

Alternatives we did not take. Refunding a charge that orders refuses keeps the race and the fees, and the buyer sees a charge and a refund. Reviving a cancelled order if its ticket is still free needs a new reservation from the tickets service (ADR 0005) and makes cancelled no longer final. Authorizing first and capturing after orders agrees avoids both, but needs Stripe's capture flow and a status machine for the authorization; the claim gives the same guarantee with the machinery we already have. A "started" event without waiting for orders' answer would still let expiry win after the charge began.

We accepted these gaps:
- A charge Listener that fails for a reason that is not the card (Stripe unreachable for all five attempts) is dead-lettered. The order stays in payment processing and the payment pending, which nothing expires, and holds its ticket like a Pending order whose confirmation is lost (ADR 0005). Replaying the events fixes it. A timeout would close it if it proves to matter.
- There is no refund. Orders refusing a `payment:paid` is not expected any more and would dead-letter.
- The Stripe token waits in the `payments` table until the charge and stays there afterwards.
- A decline close to the deadline cancels the order at once; the deadline is not extended.
- Payments' Copy of the order does not follow `order:completed`, so it rests at payment processing after a payment.
- `payment:created` events still in the topic when this was deployed have no Listener. Deploy payments before orders so the first `order:payment_processing` is not dropped.
